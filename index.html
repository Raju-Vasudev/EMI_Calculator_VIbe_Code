EMI calculator

What will my EMI be?

  1. Enter loan amount, rate and tenure
  2. Choose fixed or floating
  3. For floating, add the rate changes you expect
  4. Read your EMI, interest and schedule

How it's calculated

  1. Monthly rate r = yearly rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100
  2. EMI (reducing balance, what banks use) EMI = P × r × (1 + r)ⁿ ÷ ((1 + r)ⁿ − 1) P = loan amount, n = number of EMIs.
  3. Each month Interest = balance × r · Principal = EMI − interest · New balance = balance − principal Total interest is the sum of every month's interest.
  4. Flat rate Total interest = P × yearly rate × years · EMI = (P + total interest) ÷ n The "same as reducing rate" figure is the r that gives this EMI in formula 2, found by trial.
  5. Floating rate change at EMI k. If the EMI changes: New EMI = formula 2 with P = balance left, n = EMIs left If the EMI stays: EMIs left = −log(1 − balance × r ÷ EMI) ÷ log(1 + r)

One-time charges

What do I pay before the first EMI?

  1. Enter your loan
  2. Fill in each charge the bank quotes
  3. See the total and the true yearly rate

How it's calculated

  1. Processing fee Fixed: the amount · Percent: loan × % (then raised to the minimum or cut to the maximum)
  2. GST GST = 18% × (processing fee + documentation charges) Stamp duty, franking and RTO fees carry no GST.
  3. Total upfront Total = processing + documentation + GST + stamp duty + franking + RTO + other charges
  4. True yearly rate: you receive the loan minus the charges, but pay the full EMI. It is the rate R where EMI formula(P − total charges, R, n) = your actual EMI found by trial. This is how banks work out the APR on the Key Facts Statement.

Part payment

How much do I save by paying extra?

  1. Enter your loan
  2. Choose extra EMIs, a fixed amount, or a one-time sum
  3. Choose shorter tenure or lower EMI
  4. See the saving

How it's calculated

  1. Normal schedule as in the EMI calculator: Interest = balance × r · Principal = EMI − interest
  2. On an extra-payment month New balance = balance − extra amount An extra EMI counts as the current EMI amount. A fixed amount "3 times a year" is paid every 4 months.
  3. Keep EMI, finish sooner EMIs left = −log(1 − balance × r ÷ EMI) ÷ log(1 + r) Keep tenure, lower EMI New EMI = EMI formula(balance, r, EMIs left)
  4. Charges Charge = extra amount × charge % × 1.18 (if GST is ticked)
  5. Saving Interest saved = interest without extras − interest with extras · Net saving = interest saved − charges

Foreclosure

How much to close my loan fully?

  1. Enter your loan
  2. Pick when you want to close it
  3. Add the bank's foreclosure charge, if any
  4. See the amount and the saving

How it's calculated

  1. Balance after EMI k (read from the schedule, same as) B = P × (1 + r)ᵏ − EMI × ((1 + r)ᵏ − 1) ÷ r
  2. Foreclosure charge Charge = B × charge % × 1.18 (if GST is ticked)
  3. Amount to close Amount = B + charge The bank also adds interest for the days since your last EMI, so the real figure is a little higher.
  4. Interest avoided Interest avoided = EMIs left × EMI − B This equals total interest minus the interest already paid.
  5. Net saving Net = interest avoided − charge

Prepay or invest?

Should I prepay the loan or invest the money?

  1. Enter your loan
  2. Enter the spare money and when you'd use it
  3. Pick where you'd invest it
  4. See which leaves you richer

How it's calculated

  1. Fair comparison: both plans take exactly the same money out of your pocket every month. Only where the spare money goes changes.
  2. Prepay plan: the spare money goes into the loan, so it closes early (part payment rules). From then until the original last EMI, the EMI you no longer pay is invested each month.
  3. Invest plan: the spare money is invested, and the normal EMIs continue to the end.
  4. Interest saved Interest saved = interest without prepaying − interest with prepaying It is not added separately: Freed EMIs invested = amount prepaid + interest saved − charges
  5. Monthly growth g = (1 + yearly return ÷ 100)^(1/12) − 1 · Value next month = value × (1 + g) + new money
  6. Tax at the end. FD and debt fund: Tax = (final value − money put in) × your slab × 1.04 Equity fund: each month's money is a separate purchase. Money held over 12 months is long-term, the rest short-term: Tax = (long-term gains − ₹1.25 L) × 12.5% × 1.04 + short-term gains × 20% × 1.04 All rates come from the Tax rates box and can be edited.
  7. Family member's name: FD and debt fund tax become 0% (their slab with no taxable income). Equity keeps the long-term and short-term rates, because the ₹12 lakh rebate does not apply to them. If the basic exemption box is ticked: Taxable short-term = short-term gains − ₹4 L · Taxable long-term = long-term gains − ₹1.25 L − any ₹4 L left
  8. Money in hand In hand = final value − tax compared on the date your loan was originally due to end, when the loan is fully paid in both plans.
  9. Break-even return: the return at which both plans leave the same money in hand, found by trial. Below it, prepaying wins.

Compare banks

Which offer costs least overall?

  1. Enter the loan and tenure
  2. Fill in each bank's rate and fees
  3. Test a rate rise and a prepayment
  4. See the cheapest

How it's calculated

  1. Each offer runs through the same EMI schedule for the same amount and tenure.
  2. Floating offers switch to (rate + rise) from the chosen EMI, keeping the tenure: New EMI = EMI formula(balance, new r, EMIs left) Fixed offers keep their rate.
  3. Prepayment: the lump sum is paid after the chosen EMI and the loan finishes sooner. Prepay charge = lump sum × charge % × 1.18 (if GST is ticked)
  4. Total cost Total cost = total interest + fees × 1.18 + prepayment charge The lowest total cost is the cheapest offer.
